Coasts · MSc thesis & journal article

Shoreline change after four cyclones

Using Landsat 8 imagery from before and after each storm, I mapped how the coastline of Bangladesh moved after cyclones Sitrang, Bulbul, Amphan and Mocha. Stronger storms caused more erosion. Satkhira and Khulna lost the most shoreline, Bhola and Noakhali gained land through accretion, and Cox's Bazar held up comparatively well.

Project 01Coasts

Shorelines were extracted with the MNDWI water index and compared with the Digital Shoreline Analysis System (DSAS), which measures net shoreline movement along transects in metres.

Geospatial Technology for Rapid Shoreline Change Analysis of Tropical Cyclones in Coastal Bangladesh

Pobon, M. T. A. N., Akter, R., and Mahmud, K. H. Submitted to The Jahangirnagar Review. Preprint on Research Square, November 2025 · doi:10.21203/rs.3.rs-7954742/v1

MSc thesis

Geo-spatial Technology for Rapid Eco-Physical Impact Assessment of Tropical Cyclones of the Bay of Bengal

  • Measured the physical impact of multiple cyclones with multi-temporal GIS and remote sensing, using shoreline erosion and deposition as the main indicator alongside vegetation change.
  • Quantified shoreline change for four cyclones (Sitrang, Bulbul, Amphan and Mocha) using Landsat 8 imagery, MNDWI and the Digital Shoreline Analysis System (DSAS).
  • Compared impacts across cyclone categories to gauge relative severity.
  • Zoned coastal districts by impact, giving a spatial framework for risk management.
