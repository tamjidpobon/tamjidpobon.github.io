LegendAbout

Spatial analysis for climate hazards and the people who live with them.

I work where Earth observation meets community research. I measure how extreme events change the land, from shoreline erosion after a cyclone to shifts in vegetation, and I pair that with participatory fieldwork to understand risk, vulnerability and adaptation on the ground.

Since 2024 I have applied the same methods in practice at the Center for Environmental and Geographic Information Services (CEGIS), on national projects ranging from groundwater stress to the Economic Census.

  • Risk, vulnerability and community

    Community resilience, adaptation, participatory research, flood risk, and hazard-related mobility and evacuation.

  • Change during extreme events

    Tropical cyclone impacts, shoreline erosion and deposition, vegetation change, coastal and fluvial geomorphology, sediment dynamics.

  • Methods

    GIS, remote sensing, Earth observation and spatial modelling, combined with participatory and survey-based field methods.

Layer 02Ongoing

Ongoing work

Journal article · Main author Submitted to NHESS · Oct 2026

Spatial transferability of knowledge-based, statistical and machine learning models for flood susceptibility mapping in Bangladesh

With Khandakar Hasan Mahmud. Building on my BSc dissertation, this study compares an expert-based model (AHP), a frequency ratio model and three machine learning models for mapping flood susceptibility across Bangladesh, using ten conditioning factors and flood records from the Global Flood Database. It tests how well each model predicts in regions it was not trained on, and finds that the simpler frequency ratio model transfers better than machine learning while staying transparent.

Research in progress In progress

Integrating flood hazard and road-network exposure to map flood risk to urban roads: a Fuzzy AHP approach in Dhaka, Bangladesh

Combines a flood hazard model with the exposure of Dhaka's road network to show which city roads face the greatest flood risk, using Fuzzy AHP to weight the factors.

Journal article · Sole author In preparation

Socio-economic conditions and perceived microclimatic stress among tea garden workers: a case study of Khadimnagar Tea Estate, Sylhet

Based on surveys with tea garden workers during my master's fieldwork in Sylhet, this study looks at wages, housing and support, and at how workers experience heat and humidity in the gardens. It finds an overlapping burden of low pay, poor housing and heat exposure at work, and points to wage reform, better housing, shade trees and heat-safe work hours.
